# DESIGN PREMISE

Students are not interviewing people.

Students are investigating an organisation.

Stakeholders are vehicles through which students discover:

- evidence
- assumptions
- conflicts
- incentives
- organisational realities

The objective is not to discover a correct answer.

The objective is to produce a defensible recommendation under uncertainty.

---

# ORGANISATIONAL POLITICS MAP

CEO
Supports transformation if business capability improves.

Finance Director
Supports transformation only if implementation risks are understood.

Chief Design Engineer
Supports capability growth but fears productivity loss.

Manufacturing Director
Will oppose any strategy that threatens delivery.

Quality Manager
Will oppose any strategy that creates compliance uncertainty.

IT Manager
Increasingly concerned about lifecycle and supportability.

HR Manager
Concerned with capability development and recruitment.

Airbus Representative
Focused on long-term digital capability.

Senior CAD Engineer
Quietly favours retaining CATIA-derived workflows.

Graduate Engineer
Favours newer digital engineering environments.

# INFORMATION OWNERSHIP MATRIX

Students should not obtain all information from one stakeholder.

Finance owns:
- budget realities
- investment governance
- ROI expectations

Manufacturing owns:
- workflow dependencies
- production impacts
- CAM consequences

Quality owns:
- AS9100 implications
- audit impacts
- approval processes

HR owns:
- recruitment realities
- skills pipeline
- training implications

IT owns:
- lifecycle support
- cybersecurity concerns
- infrastructure implications

Airbus owns:
- future customer expectations
- collaboration requirements
- perception of supplier capability

CEO owns:
- strategic direction
- board priorities
- competitive positioning

# CONTRADICTION LIBRARY

These contradictions are intentional.

Students should discover both sides.

## Cost vs Capability

Finance:
'Large transformation programmes create substantial risk.'

Airbus:
'Failure to evolve may create even greater strategic risk.'

---

## Productivity vs Transformation

Chief Engineer:
'Migration will reduce productivity.'

CEO:
'Stagnation may be more damaging than temporary disruption.'

---

## Stability vs Future Readiness

Senior CAD Engineer:
'Existing workflows work well today.'

Graduate Engineer:
'Today's workflows may not suit future engineers.'

---

## Compliance vs Agility

Quality:
'Governance must be protected.'

Operations:
'Overly cautious approaches can slow improvement.'

---

# ASSUMPTION LIBRARY

Students should identify assumptions.

Examples:

- New software automatically improves productivity.
- Existing workflows can be recreated easily.
- Engineers will accept change.
- Customers value the same capabilities they valued five years ago.
- Recruitment challenges can be solved through training alone.
- Technology risk is larger than organisational risk.

---

# CONSULTING QUESTION FRAMEWORK

Low-quality questions:

- What concerns you?
- What do you think?
- What would you like?

Higher-quality questions:

- What assumptions are people making?
- What evidence would change your view?
- Which risk is being ignored?
- What could cause this strategy to fail?
- Which stakeholder would disagree with you?
- What information are we missing?
- What would worry you if you were the CEO?
- If resources were unlimited, what would you recommend?
- If no change occurred for five years, what happens?
- What appears attractive but is actually dangerous?

---

# CHALLENGE QUESTIONS

Stakeholders should occasionally challenge students.

Finance:

'Convince me this is not simply an expensive technology upgrade.'

CEO:

'How does your recommendation improve competitive capability?'

Quality:

'What evidence supports your confidence in compliance continuity?'

Manufacturing:

'What happens if implementation impacts production delivery?'

Airbus:

'How well will this recommendation serve AeroTech in ten years rather than one?'

---

# INVESTIGATION PATHWAYS

Multiple paths should reveal similar evidence.

Example:

Evidence:
Implementation cost may be underestimated.

Can be discovered through:

- Finance interview
- Transformation report
- Lessons learned review
- Previous project review

Students should never be forced into one dialogue path.

The board should assess:

4. Assumptions Challenged
5. Risks Identified

7. Strategic Alignment

9. Customer Consideration
