# AeroTech 2030
## Advanced Content Library for MSc Engineering Business Management

### Purpose
This document supersedes previous content libraries.

Students are operating as a consulting team reporting to the Board of AeroTech Precision Ltd.

The simulation should resemble a strategic technology adoption programme, not a role-playing game.

Questions should feel like management consulting, systems engineering, change management and technology strategy discussions.

Stakeholders should rarely reveal critical information immediately.

Students should uncover information through:

- Follow-up questioning
- Challenging assumptions
- Exploring trade-offs
- Comparing viewpoints
- Identifying contradictions

---

# QUESTION DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Avoid:

- What worries you?
- What concerns you?
- What do you think?

These are too generic.

Instead encourage:

- What assumptions are being made?
- Which risk is most likely underestimated?
- What would make this option fail?
- Which stakeholder would disagree with you?
- What information would you need before approving this?
- Where are the hidden costs?
- What dependencies are people ignoring?

The best questions should reveal intersections between:

- Technology
- Business
- Operations
- People
- Governance

# CEO

## Key Perspective

The CEO is not buying software.

The CEO is buying business capability.

## Sophisticated Question Set

Q: What business problem are we actually trying to solve?

A:
Many discussions focus on software features. The Board is more concerned with maintaining competitiveness and long-term capability.

---

Q: What would make this decision a success five years from now?

A:
Success would be reflected in contract wins, engineering effectiveness, workforce capability and resilience rather than software deployment itself.

---

Q: Which stakeholder group is most likely to underestimate this challenge?

A:
Teams often underestimate organisational change because it is harder to quantify than technical implementation.

---

Q: What are competitors doing?

A:
Many aerospace organisations are investing heavily in digital engineering capabilities and more integrated engineering environments.

---

Q: What would make the Board reject every proposal presented?

A:
If teams fail to demonstrate understanding of implementation risks, the Board may defer action entirely.

---

Hidden Evidence

Strategic Driver:
Maintaining competitive capability is more important than preserving current workflows.

Strategic Driver:
The decision concerns organisational capability rather than CAD software alone.

# FINANCE DIRECTOR

## Key Perspective

Finance evaluates uncertainty, not software.

## Sophisticated Question Set

Q: Which costs are easiest to underestimate?

A:
Lost productivity, implementation support and process redesign frequently exceed expectations.

---

Q: What assumptions in this business case would concern you most?

A:
Claims that benefits will appear quickly without operational disruption usually deserve careful scrutiny.

---

Q: Would a lower-cost solution necessarily be preferable?

A:
Not if it weakens long-term organisational capability or creates future transition costs.

---

Q: How should uncertainty influence the recommendation?

A:
Decision makers often focus on expected outcomes while underestimating downside scenarios.

---

Q: What financial question has nobody asked yet?

A:
How much of the programme budget is actually funding technology versus organisational change.

---

Q: How would you challenge a consultant recommending NX?

A:
Show me how long the implementation will take and how productivity losses will be managed.

---

Q: How would you challenge a consultant recommending CATIA retention?

A:
Demonstrate that future capability requirements can still be met.

---

Hidden Evidence

Constraint:
Major programmes compete with other strategic investments.

Risk:
Short-term operational disruption may outweigh early projected financial benefits.

# MANUFACTURING DIRECTOR

## Key Perspective

Engineering can tolerate disruption.

Factories often cannot.

## Sophisticated Question Set

Q: Which migration risk would keep you awake at night?

A:
Discovering after implementation that critical manufacturing knowledge was embedded in unofficial workflows.

---

Q: Why might engineering teams underestimate manufacturing impacts?

A:
Because much of manufacturing capability sits within processes rather than software tools.

---

Q: What assumptions are dangerous?

A:
Assuming every existing workflow can be recreated easily in a new environment.

---

Q: How would success be measured?

A:
Minimal production disruption, reduced inefficiency and improved engineering-manufacturing integration.

---

Q: Which platform characteristic matters most?

A:
The quality of integration across the engineering and manufacturing lifecycle.

---

Hidden Evidence

Risk:
Validation effort may exceed expectations.

Constraint:
Production commitments cannot simply be paused during implementation.

Opportunity:
Improved digital continuity could reduce manufacturing waste.

# QUALITY MANAGER

## Key Perspective

Every change creates governance implications.

## Advanced Questions

Q: What hidden work do engineering teams often overlook?

A:
Updating procedures, training records, governance controls and audit evidence.

---

Q: What would failure look like from a quality perspective?

A:
Losing confidence in configuration control and engineering traceability.

---

Q: Why should quality influence software selection?

A:
Because capability without governance introduces organisational risk.

---

Hidden Evidence

Constraint:
Regulatory obligations remain unchanged regardless of platform.

Risk:
Governance redesign effort could exceed expectations.

# AIRBUS REPRESENTATIVE

## Key Perspective

Customers purchase capability, reliability and confidence.

## Advanced Questions

Q: What capability trends matter most?

A:
The ability to collaborate efficiently across increasingly digital supply chains.

---

Q: Would software choice influence supplier selection?

A:
Not directly. The capability enabled by that environment may influence future competitiveness.

---

Q: What concerns customers during transformation programmes?

A:
Delivery performance often matters more than technology ambition.

---

Q: Which assumption should AeroTech challenge?

A:
Assuming current customer expectations will remain unchanged for the next decade.

# DETAILED CONSEQUENCE MODEL

## STAY WITH CATIA V5

### Year 1

The Board approves a limited-risk strategy.

Engineering teams appreciate the stability.

Productivity remains largely unchanged.

No major retraining programme is required.

Finance reports minimal disruption.

However several Board members question whether difficult decisions have simply been deferred.

### Year 3

Support and lifecycle concerns become more visible.

Some experienced engineers retire.

Recruitment becomes increasingly difficult because fewer new engineers possess significant CATIA V5 experience.

Customers begin asking more questions about future digital engineering capability.

### Year 5

AeroTech remains operationally stable but increasingly constrained.

A large transformation programme is now unavoidable.

The organisation faces a more complex transition than would have been required earlier.

Board Verdict:
Defensible short-term management. Weak long-term strategy.

---

## 3DEXPERIENCE

### Year 1

Implementation complexity exceeds initial expectations.

Some engineers become frustrated by changes to established workflows.

 Programme governance consumes significant management effort.

 Productivity temporarily declines.

### Year 3

Engineering, manufacturing and programme teams begin seeing benefits from improved information continuity.

 Digital collaboration improves.

 Configuration management becomes more robust.

### Year 5

 AeroTech presents itself as a digitally mature engineering organisation.

 New programme opportunities emerge.

 The organisation is better positioned to participate in model-based engineering initiatives.

 Board Verdict:
 Difficult implementation. Significant strategic benefits.

---

## SIEMENS NX

### Year 1

 Training requirements prove substantial.

 Some migration challenges exceed expectations.

 Engineering and manufacturing teams spend significant effort adapting workflows.

### Year 3

 Strong integration benefits emerge.

 Several inefficient hand-offs between engineering and manufacturing are eliminated.

 Teams report improved continuity between design, simulation and manufacturing activities.

### Year 5

 AeroTech is recognised as having strong engineering and manufacturing integration.

 Future capability improves.

 Some stakeholders continue to debate whether implementation effort was underestimated, but few wish to reverse the decision.

 Board Verdict:
 High-risk transition that ultimately delivered meaningful capability improvements.

---

## CREO

### Year 1

 Transition proves manageable.

 Organisational disruption remains lower than feared.

### Year 3

 Benefits emerge steadily.

 Improvements are incremental rather than transformational.

### Year 5

 AeroTech remains competitive.

 Questions remain about whether greater strategic ambition would have created additional value.

 Board Verdict:
 Balanced decision with moderate risk and moderate reward.

# DEBRIEF ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

The simulation should evaluate:

1. Evidence completeness
2. Stakeholder coverage
3. Contradictions identified
4. Assumptions challenged
5. Risks identified
6. Constraints identified
7. Workforce implications considered
8. Customer implications considered
9. Recommendation strength
10. Mitigation quality

The highest scoring teams are not those that select the best platform.

They are the teams that demonstrate the strongest investigation process.
